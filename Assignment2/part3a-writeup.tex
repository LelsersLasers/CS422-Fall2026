\section*{Summary}

Across five public iperf3 servers and three repetitions, \textbf{BBR had the
highest per-run mean goodput in all 15 destination--repetition comparisons}
(78.1\,Mbit/s mean vs.\ 52.1 for CUBIC and 43.6 for Reno). These overall
means average successful tests; Reno has one fewer test, so the paired
comparisons are more informative. BBR's destination-level mean RTT was
8.7--14.4\,ms above CUBIC and 10.4--19.3\,ms above Reno; its retransmitted-byte
fraction averaged about 11\%. CUBIC beat Reno in 13 of 14 matched comparisons.
The traces are consistent with loss/recovery limiting CUBIC and Reno, but do
not establish the sole bottleneck. A separate, shorter Docker rerun reported
below changes the rankings, illustrating the limits of generalizing from
these public paths.

\section*{Setup}

Part 3 reuses the five destinations from Part 1 (minimum RTT in
parentheses): LeaseWeb US (48\,ms), FortiSASE ES (105\,ms), MilkyWan FR
(109\,ms), Multinet24 PL (130\,ms), and Mauritius Telecom (288\,ms). In each
repetition, every destination runs all three algorithms back to back, and the
order rotates: CUBIC$\rightarrow$Reno$\rightarrow$BBR, then
Reno$\rightarrow$BBR$\rightarrow$CUBIC, then BBR$\rightarrow$CUBIC$\rightarrow$Reno.
Each test is preceded by a 3\,s pause, and refused tests are retried up to
twice. One test (Reno, repetition 3, LeaseWeb) was refused every time, so
44 of 45 tests completed. The algorithm is set with
\texttt{setsockopt(TCP\_CONGESTION)} before connecting and verified with
\texttt{getsockopt}.

\textbf{Metrics} (all from TCP\_INFO on the data socket): goodput from
$8\Delta\texttt{bytes\_acked}/\Delta t$, using actual elapsed sampling time;
smoothed RTT; excess-delay proxy $=$ mean RTT $-$ \texttt{min\_rtt}; and loss
proxy $=$ retransmitted bytes / TCP bytes sent. The plots use retransmitted
segments per interval. Retransmissions are not a direct packet-loss
probability, and excess RTT does not isolate router queueing from wireless,
host, or VM effects. Goodput summaries are arithmetic means of sampled rates;
SD is the sample standard deviation of the per-run means. The minimum RTTs
in destination labels describe representative path baselines, not identical
RTTs in every algorithm/run.

\section*{Analysis}

\textbf{Traces (Figure~\ref{fig:ts}).} After startup, Reno grows gradually
and shows a marked window reduction near 40\,s. CUBIC repeatedly reduces and
regrows its window. Reno normally adds approximately one segment per RTT
in congestion avoidance and targets a half-window threshold on a loss event;
CUBIC uses time-based growth and a nominal 0.7 decrease factor. Neither
reduces independently for every retransmitted packet in a burst. BBR uses a
larger window and has repeated brief dips near 12, 31, and 48\,s, consistent
with ProbeRTT behavior; we did not log its internal mode to confirm this.
Its sustained retransmission activity is visible in Figure~\ref{fig:scatter}.

\textbf{Operating regime.} On the four paths with baseline RTTs of
48--130\,ms, CUBIC and Reno averaged roughly 530--610 segments. When
congestion-window limited, the approximate supported rate is
$8\,cwnd\,MSS/RTT$, with RTT in seconds and MSS in bytes. A similar window
therefore supports less throughput at larger RTT. Window reductions and
slow recovery are consistent with loss/recovery limitations. However,
receiver windows, pacing, server load, and changing capacity can also limit
goodput; retransmissions alone do not establish a congestion-loss rate or
prove the bottleneck. BBRv1 primarily models delivery bandwidth and minimum
RTT rather than applying Reno-style loss-driven growth, but still handles
loss recovery and can respond to a detected policer.

\textbf{Which algorithm is better where.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Throughput: BBR}, by 1.5--1.8$\times$ Reno on the
  48--130\,ms paths and 2.1$\times$ at 288\,ms. On a long path, a loss-based
  algorithm needs more time to regrow a large window after backoff. This
  supports BBR for bulk throughput on these tested paths; it does not
  establish that their losses were non-congestive or that BBR wins generally.
\item \textbf{CUBIC vs.\ Reno: CUBIC on high-BDP paths.} The gap was
  1.05--1.15$\times$ up to 130\,ms but 1.48$\times$ at 288\,ms (window 834
  vs.\ 565). CUBIC backs off less (to 70\%, not 50\%), and its growth depends
  on time since the last loss rather than on RTT, whereas Reno would need
  over 2 minutes to regrow 500 segments at 288\,ms.
\item \textbf{Delay and retransmissions: CUBIC and Reno.} Their grouped
  excess-delay proxies were about 22--29\,ms. BBR had greater mean RTT on
  all five destinations. Retransmitted-byte percentages were
  0.04--0.52\% for CUBIC, 0.36--1.01\% for Reno, and 7.39--15.51\% for BBR.
  BBRv1's probing and larger window targets are consistent with these
  observations, but cwnd is a limit, not the measured bytes in flight, and
  we did not measure bottleneck buffer occupancy.
\item \textbf{BDP.} The BDP implied by achieved goodput is about
  0.5--0.9\,MB on the shorter paths and up to 1.5\,MB in an individual
  long-path run. The code computes median achieved goodput times minimum
  RTT, divided by eight for bytes. This is a goodput-based BDP proxy, not
  independently measured capacity BDP. Converting cwnd to bytes requires
  the recorded MSS. Thus the observed window differences support a
  throughput/RTT interpretation but do not prove underfilling or buffer
  overflow relative to the true path BDP.
\end{itemize}

We did not control buffer depth, capacity, or background traffic, so these
tests do not isolate deep-buffer or uncongested-capacity regimes. BBR can
have lower delay in other conditions; the separate rerun below demonstrates
that the ranking is sensitive to the measurement setting.

\textbf{Variability.} On the same destination, results were consistent:
the coefficient of variation of per-run goodput was 1.5--9.4\% for 13 of 15
destination--algorithm pairs, and up to 18\% on the long Mauritius path
(Figure~\ref{fig:var}). Across destinations, goodput varied 3--4$\times$, so
the path matters more than the repetition. The ranking held regardless of
test order: BBR was fastest in all 15 groups, and CUBIC beat Reno in 13 of 14
(the exception was a 0.9\,Mbit/s difference).

\textbf{Limitations.} Public paths change between tests, and three
repetitions is a small sample. We tested one flow at a time, so we could not
measure fairness or BBR's effect on competing flows. The bottleneck location
and buffer size are unknown, and our VM on Wi-Fi can add jitter. True
capacity BDP is unknown. TCP\_INFO sampling every 0.2\,s averages short
bursts. These measurements use BBRv1; its loss behavior and results must not
be generalized to other BBR versions. The original archive contains
per-run summaries and plotted traces, but not the original interval CSVs;
its pooled quantiles cannot be independently reconstructed from summaries.

\section*{Separate Docker rerun: four destinations, two repetitions}

On October 21, the full pipeline was rerun with:
\begin{lstlisting}
./scripts/docker.sh run -n 4 -d 20 -i 0.1 -r 2 --algorithm cubic --seed 42
\end{lstlisting}
The manifests record Linux \texttt{6.18.50}; the representative metadata
records x86\_64 and little-endian TCP\_INFO decoding. Part 1 completed 4/4
destinations in five attempts after \texttt{iperf.online.net:5209} refused
negotiation. Part 3 reused those four host/port pairs, used 20\,s transfers
and 0.1\,s sampling for every algorithm, and completed 24/24 tests, with no
skipped algorithms or failed Part 3 attempts. Its two orders were
CUBIC--Reno--BBR and Reno--BBR--CUBIC. Full raw traces, server results,
inputs, metadata, and PDFs are preserved in \texttt{rerun-results/}.

\input{rerun-results/goodput-table.tex}

Reno had the largest mean of per-run goodputs on all four destinations,
although its Mauritius result was effectively tied with BBR
(62.38 vs.\ 62.35\,Mbit/s). BBR had lower grouped mean RTT than both other
algorithms on LeaseWeb and Mauritius; on Multinet24, Reno had the lowest
mean RTT. On the representative Multinet24 path, all six Part 3 tests
recorded zero retransmitted segments. CUBIC averaged about 1,424 segments,
Reno 4,019, and BBR 5,611, with mean RTTs of 141.0, 136.4, and 138.6\,ms.
This illustrates why a larger cwnd alone need not imply higher goodput:
pacing, actual flight size, startup, and other limits also matter.

Most rerun tests had no retransmissions, so a universal loss-limited
interpretation would be unsupported. Within-destination goodput
coefficients of variation ranged from 1.1\% to 43.3\% across the two
repetitions. Two short tests are insufficient to establish steady-state
behavior or a reliable universal ranking. Host/kernel, duration, sampling,
destinations, and measurement time differ from the earlier batch; we cannot
attribute the ranking change to any one factor. Both batches support the
need to discuss variability and qualify operating-regime claims.
